## Page 1 — Recommendation and Quality of Earnings

### 1. Recommendation

**Proceed to confirmatory diligence — do not submit a binding offer until the four conditions in
§10 are met.** SMCI is a genuine hyperscale-AI infrastructure beneficiary — revenue grew from
$7,123M (FY23) to $21,972M (FY25), a ~3.1x increase in two years. But this is a **low
earnings-quality, cash-consumptive, governance-impaired** asset. We would underwrite it on **cash
conversion, not reported EBITDA**, price it on the lower of two defensible EBITDA bases, and place
the governance risk into deal structure rather than into a headline discount. The single most
important fact in this file: in FY2024 the company reported **+$1,153M of net income against
−$2,486M of operating cash flow.**

### 2. Basis of preparation and limitations (read first)

This is a **public-information QoE.** We have no trial balance, no management access, and have
performed **no revenue cut-off or inventory-aging testing.** Where public data cannot support a
quantification, we have sized the exposure and converted it into a specific confirmatory procedure
(§10). Accordingly, several issues below are deliberately **not** booked into our adjusted EBITDA —
that restraint is a feature of the analysis, not a gap in it.

### 3. The EBITDA bridge (FY2025)

| | $M | Basis |
|---|---:|---|
| GAAP EBITDA (Operating income + D&A) | **1,294** | sourced |
| + Stock-based compensation | +314 | **contested — shown both ways** |
| + Incremental audit fees vs FY22 baseline | +4 | accepted (sourced floor) |
| **Diligence Adj. EBITDA — SBC added back** | **1,612** | upper basis |
| **Diligence Adj. EBITDA — SBC as real cost** | **1,298** | lower basis |

Three points the Committee should take from the walk:

- **SBC is the swing factor, and we refuse to book it one way.** The add-back is worth **$314M of
  EBITDA — roughly $4.7B of enterprise value at 15x** (multiple is a labelled assumption). We
  present both bases and insist that whatever multiple is applied be struck on the **same basis** as
  the EBITDA it multiplies. SBC is also dilutive: it is a real, recurring economic cost, not a free
  add-back.
- **We add a cost back the other way.** Remediating the control failure (§8) requires a permanently
  larger, compliant finance function. A properly controlled SMCI carries a **structurally higher G&A
  run-rate** than it ever has. We hold this as a negative sensitivity ($15–40M/yr, benchmarked) and
  tie it to the Day-1 integration budget in §10 — the same number in both places.
- **We reject management's "non-recurring ramp cost" add-backs.** In a business that has "ramped"
  for several consecutive years, expedite freight and ramp inefficiency are the cost of operating.

### 4. Purchase-price implication

At an illustrative 12–18x (labelled assumption), the two EBITDA bases and the ramp of adjustments
produce an enterprise-value **range**, not a point. The distance between management's marketed,
SBC-added-back number and our lower, SBC-as-cost number is on the order of **$4–5B of enterprise
value** — which is precisely what the confirmatory diligence in §10 is worth. **We would not pay to
management's basis.**

## Page 2 — Working Capital and Cash Conversion (the core finding)

### 5. Reported profit does not convert to cash

| $M | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Net income | 640 | 1,153 | 1,049 |
| Operating cash flow | 664 | **(2,486)** | 1,660 |
| EBITDA margin | 11.2% | 8.3% | 5.9% |
| Accruals ratio (Sloan) | n/a | **+53.9%** | (5.1%) |

Two independent earnings-quality reads point at the same year. The **accruals ratio hit +53.9% in
FY2024** — net income exceeded operating cash flow by more than half of total assets, an extreme
reading — and the **Beneish M-score flags FY2024 (M = 0.56, above the −1.78 threshold)** while
FY2025 does not (M = −2.34). We caution the Committee that part of the FY2024 M-score signal is
benign hyper-growth (sales doubled); the components we weight are receivables growing faster than
sales, gross-margin deterioration, and slowing depreciation. **Separately, EBITDA margin has
compressed every year — 11.2% to 5.9% — even as revenue tripled.** This is growth at falling
profitability.

### 6. Net working capital and the peg

NWC swung from $1,664M (23.4% of revenue) in FY23 to **$5,337M (35.6%)** in FY24, then to $4,915M
(22.4%) in FY25. The FY24 blow-out — an inventory build from $1,446M to $4,333M — is what drove
operating cash flow to −$2,486M; it was funded largely by a **$4,645M convertible-note issuance.**

**Peg recommendation:** do **not** accept a conventional trailing-dollar-average NWC peg. In a
business whose working capital compounds with revenue, a backward-looking dollar peg sits far below
actual NWC at close and **transfers value to the seller** — the buyer finances the seller's growth
and calls it a true-up. We would peg NWC as a **percentage of LTM revenue** (or a dollar peg with a
revenue collar), and would require **24 months of monthly** NWC data before agreeing any number;
quarter-end snapshots hide intra-quarter swings and are the dates a company is most motivated to
manage.

### 7. Cash conversion cycle

| days | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| DIO | 90 | 82 | 84 |
| DSO | 59 | 47 | 41 |
| DPO | 49 | 32 | 26 |
| **CCC** | **101** | **97** | **99** |

CCC looks stable (~100 days), but the composition is the story: **DPO fell from 49 to 26 days** —
suppliers are demanding cash rather than extending terms (consistent with the related-party and
constrained-component dynamics) — while inventory days stay elevated and 74% of that inventory is
**finished goods**. Landing the plane: **Diligence Adj. EBITDA less capex less the change in NWC**
was **−$2,557M in FY2024** and +$1,588M in FY2025. A buyer underwrites cash. **Growth here is
cash-consuming; the acquisition must be financed on a revolver and a working-capital facility sized
to the inventory build, and the deal's sensitivity to a single quarter of inventory is high.**

*Capital structure note:* after the convertible raise and equity issuance, SMCI moved from ~$504M
net debt (FY24) to **~$412M net cash (FY25)** — total debt $4,758M against $5,170M cash. The
convertibles ($4,645M) and their dilution/refinancing profile are a diligence item in their own right.

## Page 3 — Deal Risk and the Central Tension

### 8. Governance and internal control

SMCI's FY2024 10-K carries an **adverse opinion on internal control over financial reporting**
(BDO, "did not maintain… effective internal control… as of June 30, 2024") alongside an
**unqualified opinion on the financial statements.** Management concluded disclosure controls were
also not effective, and that **as of the filing date none of the five material weaknesses had been
remediated.**

We mapped the five disclosed weaknesses to the COSO framework: **all sit in Control Activities or
Information & Communication (process-level).** Yet the prior auditor, **EY, resigned mid-engagement
in October 2024 without ever issuing a report**, citing an inability to rely on management's and the
Audit Committee's representations and questioning the company's commitment to **integrity/ethics
(COSO Principle 1)** and the board's **independence from the CEO (Principle 2)** — i.e., the
**Control Environment**, which appears nowhere in the five disclosed weaknesses. The one disclosed
weakness that bridges to it explicitly names **related-party transactions.** Our read: the disclosed
remediation is necessary but arguably **not sufficient** to address what EY left over. That is a
diligence question, not an accusation.

### 9. The central tension — no restatement, yet reporting risk clearly existed

A restatement and a control failure are **different tests.** A restatement asks whether the reported
numbers were materially misstated; ICFR asks whether there was a *reasonable possibility* that a
material misstatement would not be prevented or detected. **One can answer "no" to the first and
"yes" to the second without contradiction** — an adverse ICFR opinion beside a clean financial-
statement opinion is the system working as designed, not a paradox. But "no material misstatement
was *found*" is not "none exists." The absence of a restatement tells us the errors identified did
not cross a materiality threshold; it does not tell us the controls would have caught one that did.
(The retrospective adjustments to FY23/FY22 are the **10-for-1 stock split**, not an error — we
checked; not a red flag.)

The auditor's own **Critical Audit Matters — inventory valuation and revenue recognition —**
independently corroborate the two accounts our public-data analysis flagged as highest risk.

### 10. What a buyer does with it — conditions to a binding offer

1. **Diligence (expand scope; do not rely on management reps):** full revenue **cut-off testing**;
   **inventory aging** and the E&O reserve calculation (undisclosed today — 74% finished goods); an
   **arm's-length benchmarking study** of related-party pricing (3.3%–6.6% of COGS through
   CEO-family entities; each 100bps ≈ **$7M** of EBITDA); and **24 months of monthly** financials.
2. **Price:** underwrite on the **SBC-as-cost basis (~$1,298M)** and apply a multiple struck on the
   same basis; treat everything unbooked above as downside, not upside.
3. **Structure (where the risk goes):** indemnity **escrow** sized to the exposures; **specific
   indemnities** for the regulatory/securities-litigation matters; **R&W insurance** — noting the
   insurer will almost certainly exclude the *known* accounting and regulatory matters, because known
   risks are not insurable; and **closing conditions tied to remediation milestones.**
4. **Post-close:** the compliant finance-function build is a **funded Day-1 integration priority** —
   the same $15–40M/yr we carry as a negative EBITDA sensitivity in §3. Consistency between the
   bridge and the integration plan is deliberate.

**Bottom line:** a fundamentally real business, acquired at a price and structure that reflect low
earnings quality and unresolved governance — never on management's marketed EBITDA, and never on its
representations.
